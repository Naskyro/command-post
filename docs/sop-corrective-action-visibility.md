# Corrective Action Policies in Command

An HR-CA policy has one reference and version. Its audiences read different material:

| Audience | What they read |
| --- | --- |
| Employee, including a Coordinator | The conduct standard and typical consequences, with any qualifications in the policy such as repetition, severity, intent, or substantiation. |
| Manager, Department Lead / Director, or Executive | The employee policy plus the instructions for consistent review, documentation and escalation. An SOP editing grant by itself is not supervisory authority. |
| New hire | The assigned policy in onboarding. Signing a policy and approving it for publication are separate actions. |

Publishing a policy must not issue a write-up or sign for an employee. A typical consequence is a starting point, not a disposition on an individual. A manager applying it should use the recommended response, consider relevant facts and comparable cases, and document the reason for any departure. The employee's own corrective-action record should identify the actual action and the policy involved, without exposing private supervisor instructions.

Use the same audience boundary for policy lists, search terms, PDF generation, plain text, onboarding, and the policy snapshot on an HR record. Hiding a tab, an accordion or a PDF toolbar is not a substitute for returning an appropriate payload. An employee must never receive a supervisor PDF embedded underneath an overlay. Keep original imported sources restricted. Keep version history and signed receipts separate from current publication state.

**Publishing.** In the Draft Workshop, filter by topic or readiness, select drafts on one or several pages, and choose Review & Publish. Check the policy codes, versions and audiences, then approve under the signed-in editor's name. A conflicting change must stop the entire batch. Employees receive an in-app reminder to acknowledge the new version. Publication does not send external messages.

**Acknowledging.** From My Acknowledgments or employee onboarding, open each assigned policy version. Returning to the list should preserve that progress. Auto Sign All asks for an electronic signature and confirmation of individual review; it issues a separate dated receipt for each edition. An opening alone is not a signature or proof that the material was read. A new policy version requires new review.

**Regression scenarios.** Exercise an employee, a Coordinator with an SOP editing grant, a Manager, a Department Lead, a new hire, and a user whose authority changed after signing. Check the record, search, printed PDF, and original-source link. Confirm both that the employee retains meaningful access to the conduct and consequences and that supervisor-only text cannot be recovered from their received data.

**Citing policies in HR.** A supervisor can cite published SOPs from any topic they may read in a Corrective Action or Performance Note. A policy does not become available to the supervisor merely because they can write the HR record. Store the policy ID, code, title, and published version on the record, with the primary policy first. A later SOP revision is for future records; it does not retrospectively alter the citation on an issued write-up. If a selected draft citation becomes outdated before it is first saved, direct the author to the new edition rather than silently substituting it. Access to an HR record does not independently grant access to a restricted policy.
