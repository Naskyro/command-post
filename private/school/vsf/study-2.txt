Vehicle Storage Facility Study Guide Exam 2
Terms and Definitions
1. TCSO (Travis County Sheriff’s Office) - The law enforcement agency responsible for Travis County areas not covered by the Austin Police Department (APD). For towing operations in TCSO jurisdictions, vehicles must be reported using the “Private Property Impounds & Repossession” form, available online through Microsoft.
2. APD (Austin Police Department) - APD oversees law enforcement within Austin city limits. Vehicles towed within APD’s jurisdiction, meaning from addresses APD responds to, are reported using an online system called Autura (formerly Auto Return), which processes impound reports for APD.
3. WilCo (Williamson County) - This is an abbreviation for Williamson County, a region north of Travis County that includes cities like Round Rock, Georgetown, and Cedar Park. Towed vehicles from addresses in WilCo jurisdictions also need to be reported through Autura (formerly Auto Return).
4. APD Jurisdiction - Refers to areas where the Austin Police Department has authority. Vehicles towed from these areas fall under APD jurisdiction and must be reported through Autura, an online system used to handle vehicle impound reports for APD.
5. TCSO Jurisdiction - Refers to areas where TCSO has authority rather than APD. For vehicles towed within TCSO jurisdiction, reporting must be done through the “Private Property Impounds & Repossession” Microsoft form. This applies to vehicles towed from addresses in TCSO-covered parts of Travis County.
6. WilCo Jurisdiction - Vehicles towed from Williamson County, covering cities such as Round Rock, Georgetown, and Cedar Park, are considered within WilCo jurisdiction. These impounds must be reported through Autura, which manages tow reports for Williamson County.
7. Buda Jurisdiction - Vehicles towed from the city of Buda are managed differently because Buda does not use an online reporting form. Instead, any vehicle impound report must be made by calling Buda’s non-emergency line at 512-393-7896. This ensures accurate records for tows in Buda, as Buda jurisdiction does not use the Autura system or an online reporting form.
8. Autura - Autura, previously called Auto Return, is an online system increasingly used nationwide by local governments to receive vehicle impound reports. Towing companies in areas like Austin and Williamson County must submit reports to Autura for any tows within those jurisdictions.
9. TCSO Private Property Impounds and Repossessions - This is the name of the specific Microsoft form used to report private property tows and repossessions from TCSO jurisdiction. It is required for any tows managed by the Travis County Sheriff’s Office, ensuring the towed vehicle’s status is accurately logged.


10. ADD (Auto Data Direct) - ADD is an online tool that helps Vehicle Storage Facilities (VSFs) access information on a vehicle’s ownership, lienholders, and related details. Each query has a fee: Texas VIN checks are $0.26, Texas license plate checks are $0.26, and mailing fees for 1st and 2nd certified notification letters are $15.79 each. ADD can be used directly or accessed through TowBook for easy integration with towing records.
11. Drop Fee - A $50 fee is charged if a tow truck is completely ready to transport a vehicle (fully hooked and prepared to leave), but the vehicle’s owner arrives on-site and requests the vehicle be released. In this case, the vehicle must be immediately removed from the scene by the owner.
12. No Drop Fee - If the tow truck has not fully prepared the vehicle for transport, meaning it is not yet ready to leave with the car, the truck operator must release the vehicle at no charge to the owner. The owner must still move the vehicle promptly if it’s not legally parked.
13. Courtesy Release - As a goodwill policy, some towing companies offer a “courtesy release,” allowing a property manager or owner to retrieve a vehicle without a fee. This is done as a courtesy since all tows performed are legal at the time, and such releases are not required by law. Approval of a courtesy release depends on company policy and management discretion.
14. Tow Hearing - If a vehicle owner believes their car was towed unlawfully or was overcharged, they can request a tow hearing. This hearing occurs at a Justice of the Peace court and must be filed within 14 days of the tow. This process gives the vehicle owner a formal way to dispute the tow’s legality or costs.
15. Tow Codes - In Texas, specific laws, known as tow codes, govern vehicle towing operations, ensuring vehicle owners’ rights and detailing penalties for violations. These rules can be found in the Texas Transportation Code Chapter 2308 and the Occupations Code Chapter 2303.
16. Cab Card - This TDLR-issued card proves a tow truck’s license status. When a tow truck comes to retrieve a vehicle from a VSF, the driver must present the Cab Card. It includes details like the permit type (CT, PP, IM), unit number, and vehicle identification number (VIN).
17. TDLR Tow Operator Card - Issued by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation, this card confirms a tow truck driver’s license status. Tow truck drivers retrieving a vehicle from a VSF must present this card along with the Cab Card.
18. Driver’s License (DL) - A valid state-issued driver’s license, required for all tow truck drivers. This license must be shown when picking up a vehicle at a VSF. Vehicle owner may present an ID such as a driver’s license to remove a car from a VSF.
19. Pull Out Fee - An $80 fee charged when a vehicle with mechanical issues (such as a flat tire) needs to be pulled out of a VSF’s yard by a tow truck. Vehicle owners are not allowed to make repairs on-site, so the pull-out fee covers the cost of moving the car to an accessible area outside the fence.
20. Credit Card Fee - A 3% fee applied to debit or credit card payments to cover processing costs.
21. Winch Fee - When a vehicle requires winching (being pulled by a rotating cable), an extra fee is added to the towing charges. The fee varies based on the effort and time required, and it is set by the tow truck driver according to the specific job.
22. Separation of VSF Charges and Towing Charges - TDLR mandates that VSF charges (such as daily storage or impound fees) and towing charges (like APD or TCSO private property towing) be listed separately on receipts. This distinction helps clearly differentiate storage and towing-related expenses.
23. Impound Fee - A one-time fee of $22.85, charged for securing a vehicle at a VSF. This fee covers actions like checking doors, securing windows, and running an MVR (Motor Vehicle Report) through ADD. If these actions aren’t taken, the impound fee cannot be charged.
24. Notification Fee - A $50 fee for notifying a vehicle’s owner or lienholder about the vehicle’s impoundment or storage. This fee is only charged once, no matter how many notifications are sent.
25. Daily Storage Fee - A fee of $22.85 per day (or less, depending on local limits) for each day a vehicle is stored at a VSF. This daily fee is regulated by TDLR and local governments; for example, if Austin’s city limit for storage is $22.85, this rate applies even if the state maximum is higher.
26. Release Photos - Photos documenting a vehicle’s condition at the time of release, typically taken as soon as the vehicle arrives or within 30 minutes before pickup. These photos are added to the vehicle’s record in TowBook, providing a visual record of its state upon release.
27. Parking Addendum - An extra document added to a tenant’s lease or mobile app parking permit registration that explains parking terms, including assigned spaces, fees, and vehicle rules for the property. Tenants are responsible for keeping this information current with the leasing office.
28. Public Roads - Towing from public roads requires law enforcement authorization, such as an incident managed by APD. In private cases, towing from public roads is not allowed without a police request. For private property towing, consent from the homeowner and proof of residency or authorization (e.g., a real estate agreement) are required when the vehicle blocks driveways or encroaches onto private property.
