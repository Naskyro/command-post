Vehicle Storage Facility Study Guide Exam 3
Terms and Definitions
1. Dialpad
A VOIP (Voice Over Internet Protocol) phone system that routes all incoming calls to a computer. The computer processes the call and duplicates the signal to ring multiple devices, such as laptops, phones, and tablets. Features include hold music and phone menu instructions, enabled by routing calls through a computer before reaching devices. 
2. Customer Service
The act of assisting and addressing the needs of customers to ensure a positive experience. It involves effective communication, problem-solving, and maintaining professionalism to meet or exceed customer expectations.
3. De-escalation (in VSF Operations)
A set of strategies and communication techniques used to calm tense situations with customers. This includes listening actively, speaking calmly, and redirecting conversations to reduce conflict, ensuring a smoother resolution during vehicle releases.
4. Empathy
The ability to understand and share the feelings of others. For VSF employees, it means recognizing the stress or frustration customers may feel and addressing their concerns with patience and compassion, even while enforcing rules.
5. Sensitivity
The capacity to be aware of and respectful toward the emotions and needs of others. In VSF operations, it involves recognizing the unique challenges customers may face and responding appropriately without compromising professionalism or policies.
6. Power & Control (in VSF Operations)
Refers to the inherent authority VSF employees hold over customers due to their ability to impound and release vehicles. Employees must exercise this power responsibly, ensuring fairness and adherence to regulations without exploiting the imbalance.
7. Emotional Trauma (in VSF Context)
The emotional distress or injury experienced by customers or employees during stressful interactions. Customers may feel trauma from having their vehicle impounded, while employees may experience trauma from hostile or aggressive behavior. Awareness and mitigation of these impacts are essential for effective operations.
8. Self-Care (for VSF Employees)
Practices that promote the well-being of VSF employees, ensuring they can handle the stress of their roles effectively. Examples include listening to soothing music between releases, taking lunch breaks, discussing challenging interactions with a trusted coworker, and maintaining a healthy work-life balance.
9. Accountability (for Customers)
The responsibility of customers to follow parking rules and regulations. In VSF operations, holding customers accountable ensures fair enforcement of rules and fosters compliance with community standards.
10. Social Impact (in VSF Operations)
The effects of ignoring parking rules on communities, businesses, and vulnerable populations. Lack of enforcement can result in blocked entryways, economic losses, and safety concerns. Vulnerable groups such as teens, women, children, LGBTQ individuals, and people of color may face increased risks when forced to park far from safe, well-lit areas.
11. Professionalism (for VSF Employees)
Consistent adherence to respectful and courteous behavior, regardless of customer attitudes. Employees should maintain a calm demeanor, even during hostile interactions. If a customer is belligerent or disrespectful, employees may end communication professionally, informing the customer they are welcome to call back when respectful communication can resume.
12. Authority
The right granted by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR) to impound vehicles and release them according to established laws and regulations.
13. Texas Administrative Code (85.726)
A regulation that requires VSF employees to operate with honesty, trustworthiness, and integrity. It serves as the ethical standard for all business activities within the Vehicle Storage Facility.
14. Informed Customer: The VSF employee’s responsibility to make sure the customer understands what’s happening and avoids confusion. For example, when handing a VSF 13 form, the employee explains that because the vehicle registration doesn’t match the ID, the customer will need to fill out a quick form and provide the vehicle’s VIN or license plate. This way, the customer knows why they need to do it and doesn’t guess the purpose of the form. This applies to any form given to the customer.

VSF Employee Quick Call Checklist
☐ Greet the Customer
“Parking & Towing Enforcement. How may I help you?”
☐ Ask for Vehicle Details
“Can you please provide the year, make, and model of your vehicle?”
If needed: “Where was the vehicle picked up from and when?”
☐ Confirm Vehicle is in Storage
“Yes, I do see that your vehicle is here”
☐ Explain Reason for Tow (if asked)
“It looks like your vehicle was towed for: __________.”
☐ Request Valid ID
“You’ll need a valid ID unexpired (state ID, driver’s license, passport, military ID, matricula consular).”


☐ Explain Instruments of Release
“Vehicle can be released to you if your ID name and address matches the name of the registered owner, or your name is on the vehicle insurance or if you have the title on hand even if the title is not in your name, (or guide them to tdlr.tx.gov for additional form options they can learn about)”
☐ Provide Payment Information
“The pricing is regulated by the state of texas as well as by the local law enforcement jurisdiction of the area. If you are paying with cash, the total is $_____ (must be exact change only) or $_____ with card (bank processing fee).”
☐ Card Payment Info
“If you are paying by card, the cardholder must be present. This means that if you are paying, the card must be under your name. If you are using someone else’s card, they must be present with their ID. We do not accept apple pay or virtual card payments (cash app, venmo, zelle, etc)”
Location
“I can go ahead and send a text message with the address of where your vehicle is being stored at. Is this a good phone number to receive that text? I recommend using google maps as apple maps may not provide an accurate location.”
☐ One-Hour Notice
“We require an hour’s notice to ensure a staff member is available for your release. If you don’t place an hour’s notice, you may end up waiting a full hour to receive assistance”
☐ Ask for Appointment Time
“Did you want to place your hour’s notice now, or call back at a later time whenever you’re ready? 
☐ Explain Additional Fees (if applicable)
“There’s a $22.85 daily storage fee at midnight and everyday after. A $50 notification fee will apply if the car is still here after 24 hours.”
☐ Confirm Appointment
“Okay, your dedicated appointment is scheduled for [time]. If you won’t be making it in time, call back to get a new appointment. Since pick up are by appointment only, you may be waiting up to an hour receive assistance if you arrive late. 
☐ Closing
“If you have any questions, feel free to call us back. Thank you”
